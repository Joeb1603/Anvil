# Forward into depthless dark

*[Image: Here is the heart of the Druj; black and twisted and filled with hate and fear.](https://www.profounddecisions.co.uk/empire-wiki/File:Into_depthless_dark.jpg)*

## Wicked Effigies and Twisted Monuments

The Salt Flats of Sanath are the true heartlands of the Druj. Broad plains criss-crossed by dark waterways. The ground is sodden, and the further east toward the sea one travels, the more marshy the land becomes. The forests common to the rest of the Mallum have mostly been cleared here, by centuries of ravenous industry. Only in the south-west is there any great woodland - the [lowland valleys](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#lower-saarnath) and the high [plateau](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#upper-saarnath) that each form half of the lands jointly called the Saarnath.

It rains almost constantly here, or so it seems. Even when it is not actively raining, the air is thick with moisture. The ground can turn slick and treacherous without warning, even in the valleys of Saarnath where the marshes of Sarangrave give way to broad-leafed rainforest. Visibility drops precipitously, a far cry from the relatively open sight-lines of the marshes and the flats to the south-west. Only the [Navarr](../nations/navarr/Navarr.md) and the [Varushkans](../nations/varushka/Varushka.md) are really at home here, but even they must beware of overconfidence. The Druj know these woods better than they do after all.

Thick as the air can be, the [miasma](../barbarians/Druj_miasma.md) is thicker, perhaps thicker than the Empire has ever encountered it. It has had generations to take root, to smother hope and ambition, to grind deep into the mud those unfortunate enough to be enslaved by the Druj. It preys on the minds of many soldiers, an insidious urge growing daily to simply give up, to turn back, to surrender, to cower. It requires constant vigilance by the captains, and by each soldier for their comrades, to keep the enervating atmosphere at bay.

The weather worsens as Summer wanes, and with the almost constant rain comes the [curse](../../out-of-character/rules/Curse.md) of [Spring magic](../../out-of-character/rules/Spring_magic.md). A fragment of [healing power](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Rivers_of_Life.md) falls with every drop, bringing burgeoning life without constraint, without reason or caution. No wounds, save the most egregious, will last the night. Death comes only to those unfortunate enough to fall far from their lines, or to those who are the victims of swift execution. The familiar magic flows everywhere, in all the water in the Sarangrave, and it protects barbarian and Imperial invader alike. It also touches the plants and creatures of the Mallum with supernatural vitality, but driving some of them to the edge of frenzy.

And what creatures they are! The Empire has heard of them, encountered them in the eastern forests from time to time, but just as this is the heartland of the Druj so it is home to the [deathstinger](../barbarians/Arkad.md#hupul), and the [green forest scorpion](../barbarians/Arkad.md#vikari), and the awful serpentine [coiled shrike](../barbarians/Arkad.md#kallad). These creatures seem to have little fear of humans or orcs, and are ambitious enough to try and prey upon them when the opportunity presents itself. Imperial soldiers quickly learn to fear the droning buzz of ebon-and-scarlet wasps the size of doves, the cold horror of awakening to discover a razor-clawed insect with its tail already poised to strike exploring the interior of their tent, the yell of surprise that quickly gives way to agonised screaming as a friend is condemned to a terrible death at the fangs of an arboreal assassin. The Rivers of Life do little to purge venom, or cure the poison that burns through the belly of those foolish enough to eat the ripe fruit from the wrong tree.

Into this land of death come the Imperial armies, ready to strike against the core of the Druj. They will have their work cut out for them in the months to come, but if they can seize victory... what a thing *that* would be!

## Who Brings Their End

Five armies march out of northern Sarangrave, through the [Turan Flats](../nations/urizen/Sarangrave.md#turan-flats) into the Sanath. This is the [second time](A_walk_in_shadow.md) the Empire has ventured into Sanath, and the situation in the south is very different to that they encountered in the north three years ago.

The [Black Thorns](../../mixed/government/military/Black_Thorns.md) and the [Citadel Guard](../mandates/Citadel_Guard.md) are already in place, and they are quickly joined by the [Gryphon's Pride](../../mixed/government/military/Gryphon-s_Pride.md), the [Lions of Adelmar](../../mixed/government/military/Lions_of_Adelmar.md) and the [Quiet Step](../../mixed/government/military/Quiet_Step.md) from [Ossium](../nations/varushka/Ossium.md), and [Semmerholm](../nations/dawn/Semmerholm.md), and [Zenith](../nations/urizen/Zenith.md). The Quiet Step bring word that the [Axou](../foreign-nations/Axos.md) of [Thornfen](../nations/urizen/Sarangrave.md#thornfen) are mobilizing for their [advance](Skull_beneath_the_skin.md) up to the Fens of Varreth. They will hopefully keep the path into the Salt Flats open to Imperial baggage trains, rather than devoured by ravening [Winter spirits](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Quickening_Cold_Meat.md).

When they arrive, these five are met by the [Northern Eagle](../../mixed/government/military/Northern_Eagle.md) who are already in position. The Varushkans have been busy in the Salt Flats for some time now, laying the groundwork for this invasion. There are few forces anywhere in the Empire that can match the Northern Eagle when it comes to [mastery](../../mixed/government/military/Army_qualities.md#elusive) of the wild forest, the sodden marsh, the rough dark hill. For the last six months they have been engaged in a game of cat-and-mouse with the Druj defenders of the Saarnath, avoiding any attempt to trap them or bring them to battle, and patiently putting their plans in motion.

The captains of the Eagle report an unqualified success; they have collapsed strategically chosen passes and bridges across the southern Salt Flats, destroying carefully selected caches and otherwise putting their guerrilla training to good use. This will not impede the armies of the Druj, who can carry their supplies with them, and resupply from baggage trains but for the [garrisons](../../out-of-character/rules/Fortification.md#garrison) of the Druj [fortifications](../../out-of-character/rules/Fortification.md) - the [Witness](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#the-witness), the [Spires of Rarckan](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#spires-of-rarckan), and the [Beacon of Iskrid](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#beacon-of-iskrid) - it is a different matter. This season, those garrisons are unable to intervene in Lower Saarnath, occupied clearing the damage the Northern Eagle has inflicted, limited by their ability to move provisions into the forests of the south. The Empire will still have a fight on its hand, but the forces the barbarians can bring to bear will be limited to their armies - armies already battered from fighting in Sarangrave and the [Forest of Ulnak](../barbarians/Forest_of_Ulnak.md), armies that have still not fully recovered.

The Northern Eagle have laid the groundwork, but they are not finished with the Druj. Not by a long chalk. The Varushkan soldiers are already planning their next strike against the Mallum, and the planning and execution of their strategy requires all their attention. They are still able to provide a bounty of valuable scouting reports, marking out the areas the Imperial armies should avoid, as well as places they may want to target. Then they disappear back into the rainforest. Their fight this season is very different to that of the conquerors here to fight the Druj openly; it remains to be seen what punishment they have in mind for the barbarians in the coming months.

It is for the Gryphon's Pride to step into the breach. [Conquest](../../mixed/government/military/Army_qualities.md#conquering) is their calling after all, and they press forward with a single overriding goal - to seize Lower Saarnath from the Druj and drive the armies of the Mallum before them. They lead the way, a solid spear-strike deep into enemy territory. They are closely supported by the [cunning](../../mixed/government/military/Army_qualities.md#cunning) of the Quiet Step, who focus their efforts on setting ambushes and luring enemies into traps. They remain mobile, taking advantage of every weakness in the enemy strategy to attack from the flanks. Their tactics are unorthodox but effective - poison and venom, targeting healers, magicians, and supplies. They kill without mercy, and perhaps in some way the Druj might even respect them for it.

For the most part the other Imperial armies take a more conservative approach. For those who have fought the Druj before, this caution seems the wisest course. The barbarians are fond of traps and Imperial soldiers have paid a harsh price for their overconfidence too many times in the past. The Black Thorns push forward [patiently](../../out-of-character/rules/Army_orders.md#steady-conquest), prioritising the preservation of Imperial lives over the pursuit of victory. The Lions of Adelmar complement this painstaking approach; they [stand staunchly with their allies](../../mixed/government/military/Army_qualities.md#guardian) wherever they are engaged, shields ready to block the arrows of the Druj, swords unsheathed and eager to fight alongside their comrades. Selfless and courageous, many are the Navarri and Urizeni who will owe their lives to the Dawnish once this campaign is over.

With the Lions also march a cohort of the [Knights of Glory](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Knights_of_Glory.md). They are captained by the fearsome *Elodian*, resplendent in mighty panoply of runebound steel and leading a glorious warband of hammer-smiths no less powerfully accoutred. The unstoppable blows these heavy warriors strike with their great mauls send barbarians sprawling, or smash them into trees, and both their heavy armour and these terrible strikes serves as a perfect complement to the tactics of the Lions.

Then, at last, the Citadel Guard. The [sentinels](../nations/urizen/Sentinel.md) and the battlefield magicians of Urizen are there beside their allies whenever battle is joined but their goal is clearly somewhere else. They focus on support and strengthening the soldiers of the other nations and their aid is valuable in seizing land from the Druj but actually *fighting* the barbarians is of secondary importance. While their soldiers fight, their [covens](../../out-of-character/rules/Bands.md#covens) work together on of rituals intended to gather magical power and direct it toward this unseen goal. As Summer fades the leaves on the great broad-leafed trees in their vicinity turn brown and red and yellow faster than they have any right to do so. As if something primal was being redirected in some way, out of the forests and toward... somewhere else.

There are [heralds](../eternals/Inhabitants_of_the_realms.md#heralds) of the [Summer realm](../eternals/Realm.md#summer) with the Urizeni as well - proud *Dindrayne* of the feather-brow. A war-witch with a staff of adamant, she commands a cohort of elfin heroes in mithril chain, who enthusiastically fight alongside the sentinels against the Druj. With them are a score of trumpeters, who rouse the spirits of the soldiers they fight alongside, urging them to acts of heroism and glory that are sometimes at odds with the more sober mien of the people of the southern peaks. Their commander, however, remains with the covens further back behind the lines - an uncommon position for a champion of the *Lion of Summer*.

As well as magical allies, the armies of the Empire are supported by more than twelve thousand strength at arms lead by [Imperial captains](../../out-of-character/rules/Military_unit.md), many of whom bear [enchantments](../../out-of-character/rules/Enchantment.md) of their own. The vast majority fight with the Citadel Guard, and while those versed in the arts of war are at the forefront of the campaign alongside the sentinels and battle-wizards, those with even a smattering of [realm lore](../../out-of-character/rules/Magical_skills.md#realm-lore) - especially the lore of [Spring](../../out-of-character/rules/Spring_magic.md) - are stationed further back supporting and protecting the covens. The 'Guard regularly dispatch these auxiliaries on missions deep into Druj territory, to capture resources or secure access to certain locations at critical moments.

A strong force, then, bound together by bonds of [Loyalty](../religion/Loyalty.md) and [Pride](../religion/Pride.md), of the love of comrades, and of a singular purpose: the destruction of the Druj.

## Scorpion in a Corner

That unity is not found among the barbarians of the Mallum. Even something as straightforward as a singular purpose is somewhat lacking. One would think the Druj armies would be brought together by the desperate need to keep control of as much of the Salt Flats as they can, but they show no sign of it. The armies come to defend Lower Saarnath, its true, but they do so piecemeal and that gives the Imperial armies an advantage.

The first major force to engage the Empire are the [White Lions](../barbarians/Druj_armies.md#white-lions). Named for the large venomous ants that build great nests on the Salt Flats, they are perhaps the most disciplined of all the armies of the Mallum. Some of their troops are already in Lower Saarnath, hunting the Northern Eagle, and they quickly raise a warning once it is clear the Empire has come in force. They avoid direct confrontation with the Imperial forces wherever possible, at least until the rest of the defenders start to arrive. It seems they have accepted unwise boons of [Winter magic](../../out-of-character/rules/Winter_magic.md), likely from the *[Wastewalker](../eternals/Agramant.md)*. Wounds that should kill them barely slow them down, but when they fall they die quickly. Worse, they are riven by a terrible hunger; there are disturbing scenes where warriors of the White Lions abandon their own defence to cut strips of warm flesh from the fallen - enemies and friends alike. There are handfuls of bloodthirsty [heralds](../eternals/Inhabitants_of_the_realms.md#heralds) - pale white walkers more beast than human or orc - among their number who urge them on to greater and greater excess. For all this apparent madness, however, when they are able to think clearly the White Lions prove to be a persistent and responsive threat to the Empire, seemingly always in position to counter Imperial tactics.

Over the first few weeks of the campaign, the [Hidden Widow](../barbarians/Druj_armies.md#hidden-widow), the wild warriors of the treacherous [Jagged Claws](../barbarians/Druj_armies.md#jagged-claws), and the twisted [Red Lizard](../barbarians/Druj_armies.md#red-lizard) filter in to join the defence of Lower Saarnath. They are forced to approach through the marshes of [Beladrarsh](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#beladrarsh), and by all accounts receive little assistance from the Skinweaver sept that rule that rich territory. No doubt the wealthy Skinweavers are too busy making preparations for their own survival to see the Druj armies as anything other than a wall of meat between them and the invading humans. Last to arrive are the [Black Wind](../barbarians/Druj_armies.md#black-wind), and for once they seem less interested in [tormenting](../../mixed/government/military/Army_qualities.md#cruel) the armies of the Empire and more in simply trying to stem the flood of Imperial soldiers into the Salt Flats. It is easy to forget that they hate the Druj for what they did to their people - and sometimes hard to bear that they loathe the Empire even more.

The Urizeni quickly note that none of these armies seem to bear enchantments, and that there is a distinct lack of magical wards at least in the south. This is certainly not the traditional way the Druj make war - especially in defence. Speculation runs like wildfire - has the loss of the [Tainted Basilisk](../barbarians/Druj_armies.md#tainted-basilisk) and the disappearance of the cowardly [Ghulai](../barbarians/Druj_lore.md#ghulai) of the [Palace of the Sleepers](../nations/urizen/Sarangrave.md#palace-of-the-sleepers) gutted the magical capabilities of the Druj? Or is it simply that the resources they have are focused elsewhere? Is there some other shoe waiting to drop? Is there a magical trap here, hidden behind the trees, shrouded from the eyes of the magicians and witches and [vates](../../mixed/magic/lore/Navarr_magical_traditions.md#vates)?

## Make Monsters of Us All

If the Druj are bad when they invade, they are ten times worse when forced on the defensive. Yet the air of desperation here in the dripping woods of Lower Saarnath is different, somehow. There is a tremor of fear in the people of the Mallum, it seems. They rely on purely defensive tactics, seeking to turn their admittedly superior knowledge of the terrain into advantage wherever possible. They still skirmish, but their attacks are much more focused on singular objectives - driving *this* specific band of knights away from a defensible position, overrunning *this* specific hospital and then retreating, killing *these* scouts and then fleeing without even taking the time to string their corpses up from the trees.

Afraid they might be, but they refuse to cede anything that the Empire does not wrest from their tight grasp. It is challenging for the Imperial armies to bring their weight of numbers to bear against the Druj in the confines of the rainforest; hard for them to force a large-scale confrontation when often visibility is reduced to a score of metres in every direction. When it is not the trees alone, it is the ever-present rain, or the thick, warm mists that rise on the rare occasions when the sun peaks out behind the dark clouds for a few hours. The woods favour the Druj tactics of ambush and surprise, which must be accounted for whenever the Empire advances.

Still the Druj *are* outnumbered though, and arguably outclassed. Again, though, it is the ability of the Empire's armies to trust one another than proves the greatest advantage. The Lions of Adelmar and the Quiet Step employ strategies specifically designed to aid their allies, to work with their comrades in other armies and nations. The sentinels of the Citadel Guard are adept at deploying their force where it will offer the greatest support, and then moving to another position as the ebb and flow of battle shifts. In contrast there are reports of warriors of the Jagged Claw intentionally abandoning their Red Lizard "allies" in the middle of an engagement, their deaths serving to cover a flanking manoeuvre and hasty retreat. There are incidents where the Hidden Widow are left in an exposed position by the Black Wind just to lure a band of thorns into a trap that closes around the Navarr too late to save their "comrades".

As one Dawnish troubadour marching with the Lions says, it isn't so much that the Druj are intentionally turning on one another, but rather than they seem utterly incapable of seeing anyone outside their immediate circle as being *people* at all. Rivals or pawns to be used to their own advantage, in the pursuit of their own ambitions or their own survival. A people culturally incapable of feeling the bonds of fellowship, utterly alien to the understanding of love that the Lions hold at the centre of their philosophy.

## Handfuls of Dust

A month after the fighting begins, the Empire encounters serious magical resistance. The Gryphons Pride and the Black Thorns have pushed a little ahead of their allies, driving north-east where the land slopes upward toward the great plateau of Upper Saarnath. A major force of White Lions, supported by warriors of the Hidden Widow, attempt a pincer movement and fighting erupts among the trees. At first it seems that the Pride and the Thorns cannot fail to achieve victory, but then a third force joins the fighting.

They are not an army, or even really a garrison, but rather a force of perhaps a hundred or so hooded orcs with bows and barbed spears. The Thorns identify them as [Vikari](../barbarians/Arkad.md#vikari) although their banner is unfamiliar - that of a skeletal serpent similar to the coiled shrike favoured by the Kallad. This, reason the Navarr, must be the Vileshrike sept from the Dripping Tor. They are some distance from home, presumably on the orders of the rumoured ghulai *Graknuz Acidtouch*. They are not alone, however.

With the Vileshrikes are a hundred or so beings that seem to be heralds of the [Night realm](../eternals/Realm.md#night), similar to those encountered a few months ago by Imperial champions fighting [alongside the Earthquake Drummers](../../mixed/government/military/Alongside_the_Earthquake_Drummers.md) in the Forest of Ulnak. Scarecrow-like, in billowing black robes, it is easy at first to mistake them for [tortured souls](../barbarians/Druj_lore.md#tortured-souls). Yet these creatures have never been mortal; there is nothing beneath their robes save animate darkness, shadows that seem to seep through the empty eye-holes of the featureless masks they wear. The merest touch of these awful phantoms [weakens](../../out-of-character/rules/Calls.md#weakness) the strongest knight, draining away their will to fight and overwhelming them with fear and terror. Their weapons assail the mind as well as the body, creating magical wounds that confuse the confident thorns leaving them riven with self-doubt and uncertainty. A few are armed with terrible pole-arms that drown their foes in [paralysing](../../mixed/magic/spells/Paralysis.md) wells of phantasmal horror, leaving them as easy picking for the rest of the barbarians alongside who they fight.

The Vileshrikes - if that is who this is - don't especially coordinate their attacks with the two armies but rather take advantage of their attack to cover an assault of their own. The battle-magicians are clearly experienced fighting alongside their unnatural allies - they [bind](../../mixed/magic/spells/Entangle.md) a likely target long enough for the fluttering-robed things to descend on them and assail their spirit. The Rivers of Life does nothing to bolster the broken will to fight, it cannot heal the terrible nightmares that assail those injured by the creatures' weapons, nor offer protection from the sucking quagmire of doubt that overwhelms the doughtiest warrior.

It doesn't help that these vikari are obviously backed by significant magical resources - enough for some of their number to unleash great wails that weaken enemies and allies alike, or terrifying atonal hymns that [curse](../../out-of-character/rules/Calls.md#curse) Imperial soldiers with a growing delirium that takes hold hours after the fighting when they think themselves safe.

It's hard to say for certain how much difference this additional force of Druj makes to the overall battle; for all their magical prowess Vileshrike Vikari and terrorising Night held alike are as vulnerable as any magician to [arrows and mighty blows](../../out-of-character/rules/Calls.md#impale), can be put to flight with a [solid strike](../../out-of-character/rules/Calls.md#cleave) from a skilled fighter. They also seem to have little stomach for a long fight. They expend their magic quickly, it seems, and then fall back while the White Lions and the Hidden Widow still clash with the Pride and the Thorns.

The Imperial advance is turned back that day, although the Pride and the Thorns manage to lead some of the more bloodthirsty Druj into an ambush set by the Quiet Step. Less than a week later, with the Lions and the Step to back them up, the two Imperial armies return and the Navarr and Dawnish are victorious. They continue to push through the Salt Flats, and the further north-east they travel the more common and vicious fighting with the Vileshrikes becomes.

Whoever the Vileshrikes are, whoever their allies are, they do not fight anywhere else in Lower Saarnath; only in the north-east. They also seem to be the source of a peculiar magical effect, a curse of some kind, that is slowly settling over the southern Salt Flats. Soldiers begin to report an increasing number of horrific dreams, and those standing watch at the darkest hours of the night claim to spot hooded figures watching them from the shadows who they *know* are filled with poisonous hate. It doesn't seem to be an enchantment; perhaps this mysterious ghulai is drawing on other sources of power to do... whatever they are doing.

Despite all this, the Druj are forced to retreat again and again. As the season draws on, as the Quiet Step and the Lions of Adelmar come within sight of the location marked out by Imperial scouts as the *Dripping Tor*. It lies in the depths of the forest, far from any other settlement, but it has been identified by the Northern Eagle as a key strategic location to control. When the armies arrive, however, they find the Tor... missing. In its place looms an impenetrable grove of massive, clearly supernatural trees. When any Imperial soldier gets within a mile of the rough location, thick mists boil out of the ground and seethe in the air, creating impenetrable curtains of grey and white. Those who do not turn back soon regret it - scouts report encounters with unspeakable horrors in the depths of the mists. Some who brave the fog are left shattered in mind and spirit, raving about *eyes* and *claws* and *the terrible soft music*. Others do not return at all.

The decision is made to give the area a wide berth. None of the armies here are in a position to try and penetrate whatever ward is in place, and there are still Druj to be fought. A watch is set by the Quiet Step, and the Empire turns its attention elsewhere. At least there are no further attacks by the Vileshrikes and their unnatural allies, once the armies reach the Tor.

*[Image: Jaromir Ostrovyn Kostka , General of the Northern Eagle, leads the Varushkans in subtle infiltration of the Salt Flats.](https://www.profounddecisions.co.uk/empire-wiki/File:Jaromir_Ostrovyn_Kostka.jpg)*

## For Those Who Come After

In the end, there is no single decisive battle in the valleys of Lower Saarnath, only a string of engagements spread out through the woodlands. There are relatively few Druj settlements here - and they are poorly prepared for attack by Imperial soldiers. Unless the Druj armies are present, these scattered arboreal villages and logging camps swiftly fall to the Empire. Even where the Druj defenders are present, it is usually not enough to stop the Empire seizing them anyway. Usually.

Ultimately the Empire is victorious, smashing through the Druj defences and forcing the armies to retreat. Lower Saarnath is captured in its entirety, and the Imperial forces regroup to consider their next move. The Rivers of Life help to blunt the slaughter on both sides, but when the final tally is made it is still sobering. A thousand Imperial lives lost in the woodlands to the Druj. It is scant consolation that, thanks in particular to the presence of so many Imperial captains, the barbarians seem to have paid a much steeper price. It is difficult to say how *much* of a price as always, but even with their purely defensive strategy the Druj have *suffered* this season.

As Autumn approaches, armies on both sides take stock and consider their next moves; but then -

Then something unexpected happens.

## What Brings Their End

*[Image: Andronikos of the Lighthouse-That-Was , General of the Citadel Guard, has unleashed the devastating power of Spring magic on the Druj capital.](https://www.profounddecisions.co.uk/empire-wiki/File:Andronikos_of_the_Lighthouse-That-Was.jpg)*

For three months, the Citadel Guard have been gathering strength, working carefully crafted rituals in each Spring [regio](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Regio.md) they encounter, weak or strong. They have been studying the flows of [mana](../../out-of-character/rules/Mana_site.md) across Lower Saarnath, sending out small bands of diviners and sentinel defenders to learn more about the way power pools across the Salt Flats. A week before the Autumn Equinox, their work is completed.

While it takes time to prepare, and set in motion, the [Technique of the Transcendent Claw](../nations/urizen/Highest_discipline.md#technique-of-the-transcendent-claw) is not subtle in its effects. At sunrise, the sky turns black, boiling with storm clouds. The rain turns torrential, driven by howling gales. Lightning strikes willy-nilly not only across the valleys of Lower Saarnath but across the entire southern reach of the Salt Flats. Tents are torn up into the sky, unwary folk thrown flat in the mud, branches are ripped from ancient trees creating stark openings in the canopy. Imperial soldiers are at least partially prepared, but it takes the Druj mostly by surprise.

Then, the sole elfin trumpeter that accompanies the Urizen magicians sounds a single terrible note that, impossibly, is heard throughout the lower valleys. It isn't part of the working, but it seems the knights of Eleonaris have a good sense for the dramatic... and this magic is certainly *dramatic*.

Far away from the Imperial attackers and the Druj defenders, in one of the poorest parts of the sprawling city of [Leen](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#leen) itself stands the [Beacon of Iskrid](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#beacon-of-iskrid), a fortification garrisoned not by orcs but by humans under the banner of the self-styled *Prince Tristain the Golden.* A long way from the fighting, in a city that no doubt still imagines itself beyond the reach of the Empire. That dream is shattered, decisively.

With a deafening peal of thunder, audible all the way back to Lower Saarnath, a cascade of thunderbolts rains from the sky over Leen. The gale force winds become even stronger, battering the stones of the Beacon with the force of ten Winter storms, then twenty, then thirty. The rain pours in sheets, eating away at the mortar that holds the stones together, at the foundations that allow it to stand. The destruction is not limited to the fortification itself; the surrounding buildings are likewise stricken with this devastating, hungry tempest called by the highest arts of the Urizeni magicians. A tornado reaches down from the heavens, down toward the earth, and as it comes the lightning ignites something within it and it becomes a conflagration despite the monsoon deluge.

The Druj of Leen speak in whispered, terrified tones of spirits within those winds, awful shapes of water and wind and fire that somehow poured *out* of the tornado. Creatures of the Spring realm, following this consummate magical working into the mundane world for a short time, tearing at the Beacon with the single-minded hatred creatures of the Primal Forest have for all construction. Were they really there, or do the Druj imagine them in the chaos and ruin that attends upon the power unleashed by the Citadel Guard?

And then the weather starts to clear... the tumultuous downpour turns back to *just* heavy rain. The winds lessen, ceasing entirely for a moment before returning again at more normal speed. Smoke rises from the rainforest, but the fires find no purchase in the soaking wood. The lightning ceases, the thunder stills, the clouds part and the wan Autumn sun shines through.

It shines on the uncertain faces of the Imperial soldiers, it shines on the satisfied and tired faces of the Urizeni masters, it shines on the terrified faces of the Druj. It shines also, sadly, on the Beacon of Iskrid. The [bastion](../../out-of-character/rules/Fortification.md#bastion) leans dangerously to the side, barely standing. The upper ramparts have been torn away, as if gouged by some mighty claw, and scattered with lethal force into the city below. It looks for all the world as if the slightest push would send it tumbling into the streets of Leen, crushing everything in its path. It is almost entirely devastated... almost.

The Beacon still stands - the power of the *Onrushing Storm* was perhaps blunted by the rival resonances of life and healing in the rainwaters of Sanath this season - but only just. And while it may have avoided destruction by a hair's breadth, the people of Leen cannot fail to understand what has just happened. The [Buruk Tepel](../barbarians/Druj_lore.md#buruk-tepel), the rulers of the Druj, who imagined themselves safe in their great city have been presented with a potent illustration of just how vulnerable they are. The Druj are *afraid*.

### Participation: Bad Dreams

*[Image: Rune: Diras](https://www.profounddecisions.co.uk/empire-wiki/File:Diras.png)*

- **Something is causing particularly bad dreams in Lower Saarnath**

Any character whose military unit supported any of the armies in the Salt Flats of Sanath this season may have experienced unsettling dreams. Nightmares are not uncommon when one is exposed to the Druj miasma, but these are a step above what veterans of the Mallum, or of the fight to liberate Zenith, have reported. Nightmares caused by the miasma tend to vary between individuals as much as any other dreams, but there is a suspicious commonality of elements in many of these odd dreams.

They involve being pursued or threatened in familiar places that are twisted with mud and water, or the presence of marsh-dwelling flora or fauna. Horrible phantasmal shapes eventually invade these places, or catch up with a fleeing dreamer, and begin to drown them or smother them. Dreamers awaken terrified, short of breath, and convinced that there is something leaning over them trying to steal their breath, crush their chests, or throttle them in their sleep. Nothing is physically present, and there are no signs of either intruders or assailants.

There is no benefit to doing so, but anyone who has experienced these dreams is encouraged to start the Autumn Equinox without any [hero points](../../out-of-character/rules/Heroic_skills.md#hero) or [personal mana](../../out-of-character/rules/Magical_skills.md#magician). They can still recover hero points and mana as normal - either by getting a good nights sleep at Anvil or by taking advantage of a magical method of recovery such as a [potion](../../out-of-character/rules/Potion.md), or an [enhanted](../../out-of-character/rules/Enchantment.md) area such as [Herthfire's Hospitality](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Herthfire-s_Hospitality.md) or [Chamber of Delights](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Chamber_of_Delights.md).

## Battle Opportunity : What Dreams Must Come

- **There is an opportunity for a battle in the Salt Flats of Sanath this season**

The magic of the Citadel Guard unleashed, the fury of Spring unleashed, the storm's rain unleashed. Urizeni mages have sent vast workings of magical power at the walls of the Beacon of Iskrid. Unimaginable forces lashing at the white granite bastion, tearing masonry and roofing tiles from the structure. Yet, for all the great efforts during the season of campaigning, the fortress still towers above the squalid city surrounding it. Somehow it is clinging on by the slenderest of threads.

The correct magic was cast, [the powers of spring were called forth](../nations/urizen/Highest_discipline.md#technique-of-the-transcendent-claw) from the furious stormlashed realm as intended; the covens of the army and those that aided them did all that was required. But in the end it was just not quite enough, and the full might of the arcane undertaking fell agonisingly short of what was needed to overcome the bastion. It is a grim outcome, but when something like this happens, fate has a way of helping out, and so it proves this time.

The ghulai, the Druj magicians, have been engaged in a magic of their own. Desperate to prevent the Empire from advancing, the ghulai of the Vileshrikes have prepared a trap for the Empire. They hope to force open the regio across Beladrarsh, and both Upper and Lower Saarnath, to permit Ungholt, the Delirium King, to stalk the land. This little known eternal's fiendish touch brings terror and madness, a curse that spreads through the land, turning civilised people into savage monsters. A monstrous act by the Druj in scorching the earth, a plan to turn their own subjects into a bulwark of dread that will devastate the Empire's armies as they try to attack the region. If they succeed,these regions will become terror-stricken.

There is a chance to stop them. The ghulai's curse relies on a set of hastily erected *shackles*, erected inside the shroud at Dripping Tor. Here in a vast clearing in the forest, the *Rivenglade*, are three pillars of wood and billowing cloth. Glimpsed from afar, they are ancient, decrepit things, dank and miserable as many of the Druj's own buildings are. The fabrics at their tops add a singular splash of colour, bright and vivid as if warning of danger. Each pillar is bound up with magic, built from aged weirwood planks and driven through with mithril nails.

Despite their dilapidated appearance, power courses through them, the unmistakable power of the Night realm weaving around and into the structures. Combined together, the three pillars are acting to pull together the magic of obfuscation, transformation, and emotion into something pernicious and terrible. Perhaps this is why the Druj ghulai did not enchant their armies this season, occupied as they were preparing to unleash this terrible curse.

There is a conjunction to *Rivenglade* that will enable the heroes of Anvil to travel to the Dripping Tor on the Sunday of the Autumn Equinox. A single chance to stop the Druj from unleashing the servants of the Delirium King on their own people.

### Objective: Save the Druj from the Delirium King

- **The Vileshrike ghulai are preparing to unleash the Delirium King on three regions adjacent to the Dripping Tor**
- **If they are not stopped then Lower Sarnath, Upper Sarnath and Beladrarsh will become terror-stricken**
- **Zakalwe offers his aid in stopping the curse before it can take effect**
- **Destroy the *Night Shackles* to prevent the Reign of the Delirium King afflicting the regions**

The ghulai of the Vileshrikes have raised three Night Shackles, strange weirwood pillars, shrouded with Night magic. They have spent the season pouring magical energy into the pillars, and they intend to unleash the curse shortly after the equinox. If they are not stopped, then the curse, the *Reign of the Delirium King*, will be unleashed on Lower Sarnath, Upper Sarnath and Beladrarsh. This night magic curse will inflict the depredations of [Ungholt](../eternals/Eternals_of_the_sea.md#hongerigespin-and-ungholt) on the occupants of these regions; a wave of terror will spread over the land as mortals who sleep will find their dreams invaded by their worst fears. The fear will break minds like a hurricane tearing down the weakest trees in the forest. Those who succumb will no longer tell friend from foe, lashing out at anything that comes close.

The curse will ravage the populace of the three regions, and it might be tempting to leave the Druj to it, were it not for the dangers that the ritual will create. Beladrash in particular is home to half a dozen subject septs who are forced to toil for the Druj. Thousands of orcs will be affected, some of whom are Druj in name only. Worse, the ghulai are prepared to take this terrible step because they intend to forge a weapon from the broken minds of their own people. Driven furious with fear, the inhabitants will attack any army that tries to seize the regions or move through them. The Vileshrike plan to sacrifice their subjects to create a poisoned weapon to hurl at the Empire.

The Master Strategist loathes the Delirium King and is eager to help the Empire put a stop to this terrible plan. The eternal's heralds have provided rune-marked keys, cunningly wrought devices that can be used to pierce the shrouds that surrounds each shackle. Each one employs a set of simple runic pieces - the runes of Night combined with the two mystic runes. Magicans can use the key to discern the nature of the shroud, and pierce it.

To use a key, magicians will need to select four of the runes, place them on the board and then perform [Piercing Light of Revelation](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Piercing_Light_of_Revelation.md) to activate them.
The ritual will reveal information on how many runes are correct and how many are in the correct place. The ritual can be repeated as often as needed, but each casting will take time. To succeed, the right sequence of four runes must be determined for that pillar, with each rune in the correct location. Sharp minds and clear thoughts will be needed to guide the divinations, along with capable ritualists and plentiful supplies of crystal mana or prismatic ink to power the rituals.

Once the shroud has been pierced on a shackle the construction will collapse and the night magic ensnared around it will dissipate, removing the threat of the Delirium King's curse from falling on the associated region.

#### The Shackle of Sorrow

- **Breaking the Shackle of Sorrow will prevent Lower Saarnath from becoming terror-stricken**

If the Shackle of Sorrow is destroyed, it will prevent the Reign of the Delirium King from falling on Lower Saarnath. The Empire has just spent the season conquering Lower Saarnath. The region is a beachhead that could be used to attack Upper Sarnaath and Beladrarsh. If the orcs who live here are fed to the *Night Terror*, they will become a threat to every army based here or moving through the region.

#### The Shackle of Misery

- **Breaking the Shackle of Misery will prevent Upper Saarnath from becoming terror-stricken**

Upper Saarnath is strategically vital as the gateway to [Ûrinth](../barbarians/Salt_Flats_of_Sanath.md#ûrinth), the centre of the Salt Flats and one of the few remaining Druj strongholds from which they rule what's left of their empire. The woods are desolate but not uninhabited, and barely half the orcs who live in the dense forests are true Druj. The Empire managed to rescue the Great Forest Orcs from the Grove of Woe, but there are still countless more subjects and slaves who are forced to labour in the great forests to feed the Druj war machine.

#### The Shackle of Anguish

- **Breaking the Shackle of Anguish will prevent Beladrarsh becoming terror-stricken**

Beladrash is ruled over by the Skinweavers, and could be considered less strategically vital than the other two regions. However, it is home to no less than seven septs who are forced to serve the Druj here - if *the Last Voice You Ever Hear* is given a free reign to terrorise the dreams of the people who live here, then *thousands* will die. The Empire has often struggled to convince the septs of the Mallum to favour them over the Druj, but saving the Beladrarsh septs from the Druj could be a powerful diplomatic victory.

### Opportunity: Destroy the Beacon of Iskrid

- **Urizeni magicians who are associated with the Citadel Guard could harness the energies of a Shackle to complete the destruction of the Beacon of Iskrid**
- **An Urizeni coven can perform *Thunderous Deluge* after a shackle has been broken to destroy the fortification**

> **Association with the Citadel Guard**  
> The ritual can be performed by any Urizeni ritual group with members who roleplay that they are part of the Citadel Guard, either actively serving with it, or as one of the many magicians who support it.

The Beacon of Iskrid, the seat of *Prince Tristain the Golden*, teeters on the edge of destruction - one more surge of magical energy and it will come crashing to the ground. If the Empire can pry even a single shackle loose, then the magical energy it releases could be directed into the Citadel Guard's Transcendent Claw. That would be enough to overwhelm and destroy the Beacon. The fortification would be destroyed and its garrison scattered.

Completing the Transcendent Claw will not be easy. It *needs* an Urizen Spring coven, one with a connection to the other magicians of the Citadel Guard. They will need to perform [Thunderous Deluge](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Thunderous_Deluge.md) on one of the three Shackles, **after** the shroud has been pierced. If that happens, the magicians will be able to join their efforts with the magicians in the Guard, and add their own efforts to those of their compatriots. It doesn't need to be much - one last push and the walls of the fortification will come down.

### Battlefield threat: Combat the Servants of Delirium

- **Heralds of the Night realm stalk the area**
- **They lend their aid to the Druj defending Sanath**

The forces of the Druj are accompanied at *Rivenglade* by the Night heralds referred to as the *Servants of Delirium*. ### Environment: Miasma of despair

The entire area at *Rivenglade* , is under the effect of the [Druj miasma](../barbarians/Druj_miasma.md), a pall of potent fear that blankets the lands the Druj claim dominion over. Anyone entering the area will need to protect themselves from the clawing dread or succumb to a weakening terror that creeps into their mind.

The primary effect of the miasma is that it [weakens](../../out-of-character/rules/Calls.md#weakness) anyone exposed to it who does not have the ability to [overcome](../../out-of-character/rules/Roleplaying_effects.md#overcoming-roleplaying-effects) it. The easiest method to overcome the miasma is to receive an [anointing](../../out-of-character/rules/Anointing.md), but particularly [heroic](../../out-of-character/rules/Heroic_skills.md#hero) individuals, or those in possession of certain [enchantments](../../out-of-character/rules/Enchantment.md) or [magic items](../../out-of-character/rules/Magic_items.md) may also be able to counteract the effect. In particular, those of the [changeling](../../mixed/magic/lore/Changeling.md) [lineage](../../mixed/magic/lore/Lineage.md) are able to fight the effects of the miasma, only by becoming *extremely* angry - which can cause problems all its own.

### Terror-Stricken

- **Armies attacking a terror stricken region generate 20% fewer victory points**
- **Any army fighting in a terror stricken region or moving through it suffers 10% of it's force in casualties**
- **A tenth of the civilian population will die every season**

The inhabitants of a region that is terror-stricken are assailed by supernatural fear and dread. Those who try to sleep may find their rest plagued with nightmares. In their waking hours they may see horrifying visions lurking at the edges of their vision, or simply be overcome by a nameless dread. The strongest can endure the terror, but those less able to resist slowly lose their senses, to the point where they can no longer tell friend from foe. They will turn on any they see and try to attack and kill them. The mind-numbering terror pushes everyone it touches to the breaking point, to the point where violence is never far from the surface.

Inevitably people will die as a result of this terrible curse, some will simply never waken from their terror-filled nightmares, others will be overcome with rage and driven to violence against friends and family. Even those who are able to withstand the terror risk being killed by those who succumb. The civilian population will be decimated every season, and any army fighting in a terror stricken or moving through it suffers 10% of it's force in casualties. This will be calculated at the end of the season, after all casualties from that season's campaign have been calculated. Druj armies that aren't protected from the madness, will only suffer these if they are forced to defend one of these regions.

**Kaela's Gift**  
Secured through a bargain with the Winter Archmage, the gift allows any Imperial citizen in the hands of the [Druj](../barbarians/Druj.md) to choose to die painlessly. No corpse remains for the Druj to defile, to impale, or to use to taunt the living - the body turns to dust and ashes. This gift extends across the entire eastern Empire, into the Mallum, [Brocéliande](../nations/navarr/Brocéliande.md), and [Therunin](../nations/navarr/Therunin.md).

The gift allows any Imperial citizens fighting in the [vallorn miasma](../nations/navarr/Vallorn.md#vallorn-miasma) to choose death and *dissolution* before their bodies could be infested with the vallorn's sick version of immortality.

**OOC Note:** Using the gift requires thirty seconds of appropriate roleplaying. This ability may be used by player characters captured by the Druj during an event or who is [dying](../../out-of-character/rules/Combat.md#dying) in an area of vallorn miasma; if you do so you should ensure that you mark your character as dead as soon as you can so that your egregore is aware of your death. There is, however, one drawback to this, as Imperial magicians may soon discover: anyone who takes advantage of this ability cannot be contacted with [Whispers through the Black Gate](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Whispers_through_the_Black_Gate.md).

This ability is an exception to the normal rules on using abilities while [terminal](../../out-of-character/rules/Combat.md#terminal); a terminal character *can* use this ability if they are captured by the Druj or fallen in vallorn miasma.

### A Silence Like Screaming

*[Image: Cadwell Waystone , General of the Black Thorns](https://www.profounddecisions.co.uk/empire-wiki/File:Cadwell_Waystone.png)*

- **A het is gathering a band of Druj and strange heralds to attack an outpost of the Black Thorns**

- **The *General of the Black Thorns* is [responsible](../world/Sentinel_Gate.md#prognostication) for killing *Huk'nar Skintwister***
- **There are [no innocents present](../world/Sentinel_Gate.md#imperial-law) on this conjunction; magistrates advise that lethal force is fully justified**

It is not only the armies of the Druj that are working against the Imperial advance. Individual hets are gathering their allies to them, some - admittedly - no doubt in an attempt to survive the Empire's gaze, but others to strike back and inflict pain and suffering on Imperials. One such het, *Huk'nar Skintwister*, is gathering a band of Druj [chikad](../barbarians/Arkad.md#chikad) to them, to strike at one of the temporary outposts established by the [Black Thorns](../../mixed/government/military/Black_Thorns.md). If the het survives until the end of the summit, then they will have gathered enough Druj to them that the outpost will be overrun, inflicting two hundred casualties to the army before the season's campaign begins. Once this goal is completed, the band will disperse back into the wilds of the Salt Flats. It is the responsibility of **Cadwell Waystone**, [General of the Black Thorns](../../mixed/government/military/Black_Thorns.md), to ensure that Huk'nar Skintwister is killed.

What's strange are the creatures hovering around the edges of the Druj band; creatures of shadow, similar enough to those encountered fighting alongside the Vileshrikes this season. Heralds of the [Night realm](../eternals/Realm.md#night), with billowing robes whose weapons can leave even the most confident warrior [weakened](../../out-of-character/rules/Calls.md#weakness). To make matters worse, the heralds don't even leave a corpse to be investigated; instead breaking apart into motes of darkness that quickly dissipate.

The *Chain of the Hammerwright* has been lost to a band of Druj, supported by strange heralds of the Night realm. The *Chain*, a piece of [jewellery](../../indexes/by-category/Jewellery.md) with similar properties to the [Alder's Edge](../../mixed/magic/items/Alder-s_Edge.md), had been prepared by one of Elodian's warband as a gift to one of the mortal soldiers of the Lions of Adelmar. Now lost, *Sforzando* has requested that Dame Katelyn see that it is recovered from the Druj before it is turned to some wicked use.

If it can be recovered and presented to Eleonaris during the [formal parley](../winds-of-fortune/Here_you_come_again.md#words), then all will be right with the world and the *Chain of the Hammerwright* can be bestowed upon the intended mortal, along with a comparable reward for the general in time for the Winter Solstice. If it cannot be recovered, then the hammersmith will be distraught - at least for a time - and will have to leave Elodian's warband.
