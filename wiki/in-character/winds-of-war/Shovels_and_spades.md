# Shovels and spades

*[Image: Both Bregas and Mournwolder take it poorly when someone sides with the Jotun against their neighbours.](https://www.profounddecisions.co.uk/empire-wiki/File:Yegarra_Traitor.jpg)*

## Pickaxe and Fen

When people think of [the Marches](../nations/the-marches/The_Marches.md) it is the rich farmlands of [Upwold](../nations/the-marches/Upwold.md) and [Mitwold](../nations/the-marches/Mitwold.md) they normally imagine. A patchwork of farms, hedges, fences, gates. The earthbound clouds of bleating sheep, the waving golden oceans of grain. The distant sounds of the hurdy-gurdy, of leather and wood, of clay ale-mugs clinking. Yet here is [Bregasland](../nations/the-marches/Bregasland.md), and here [Mournwold](../nations/the-marches/Mournwold.md). Are they not also the Marches? The dour mire, where they grow eels and grudges. The rolling hills and the stubborn grasses with their rich harvests of rare ores and centuries of bitterness.

It is to these latter two, mirrors of each other - one flat marsh and the other undulant like some frozen sea - that we turn our gaze. Mirrors in land, but also mirrors of war. Western warriors bent on conquest; Imperial soldiers striving to liberate their homes.

## The Bitter Hills

The [Tusks](../../mixed/government/military/Tusks.md) hold to their oath. There are enemies in the Mourn, and until they are dealt with the army will remain. They march under a new general, but they march nonetheless. The bulk of the Jotun forces are engaged elsewhere, but there are still warriors here stubbornly resisting any suggestion of retreat or surrender. Farstrider's Watch is surrounded on all sides by angry Marchers, but the Jotun will not abandon the [castle](../../out-of-character/rules/Fortification.md). Perhaps it is their oaths to Jarl Haakon that keep them here. Perhaps they seek glory, and a bridge across the [Howling Abyss](../nations/imperial-orcs/Imperial_Orcs_religious_beliefs.md#the-howling-abyss). Perhaps, just as with Fort Braydon, they seek to purge themselves of the guilt they feel for letting so many thralls die when the Empire ravaged Mournwold with plague and death. Pick one, or all, and you will find a Jotun fighter whose heart it fills. One, a dozen, a score. They fight with the furious joy of those who know they have little left to lose.

The Tusks match them, blow for blow. They treat the grim business of war with the same solemnity and intensity they treat the harvest. When there is a crop to bring in, they swing their scythes and sickles. When there is an army to defeat, they swing their pikes and their swords. Sword and shears both cut alike. Each step forward pushes the Jotun back another step, breaks their hold on the green grasses of the Chalkdowns.

> *In-character text:*
>
> > *In-character text:*
> >
> > Jacks of the Tusks! We thank Stanley of Chalkdown for his service to the Empire, and to the Mourn. Let us show loyalty to our former General, and reclaim his home and yours. March for steady conquest in the Chalkdowns. Do not advance into Greensward, that is work for the Summer. Take pride in what we have achieved. For the Mourn! PS Janon, come along and watch the scrap. Let us show you the passion of the Marches.
> >
> > Jack Ashton, General of the Tusks

It's hard to see the outcome as anything short of predetermined. With the newly replenished garrison of [Landkeeper's Bulwark](../nations/the-marches/Mournwold.md#landkeepers-bulwark) to hold the ground already taken, the Marches press the Jotun from every side. In [Alderly](../nations/the-marches/Mournwold.md#alderly) the [green walls](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Forge_the_Wooden_Fastness.md) of [Spring magic](../../out-of-character/rules/Spring_magic.md) exert a subtle influence over the hedgerows and the forest groves, over the beasts of the fields. The [Prince of a Thousand Foes](../eternals/Irra_Harah.md) does little to help the Marcher army, but his servants are there to harass and confuse those who have come as conquerors. He has little patience for the thrall-takers, and infinite patience and compassion for those they would make their property.

One warband of Jotun, hard pressed by the Tusks, make the mistake of retreating into Alderly. They are found the next morning on the edge of the forest. Strangled in a constricting hedge of bramble and bracken, thirty pairs of dead eyes staring at the dawn. All of their weapons are missing. The *Thorn of the Ancestors* kills when he chooses not to yield. It is, perhaps, a reminder to the Empire that this particular lord of the [Spring realm](../eternals/Realm.md#spring) also has claws.

Where the *Shacklesmasher* offers support at one remove, there are other supernatural forces at work in the Mournwold. The general has invited the *Firebrand* to observe the fight, and for the [heralds](../eternals/Inhabitants_of_the_realms.md#heralds) of [Janon](../eternals/Janon.md), to watch is to do. Twenty wild warriors, wrapped in scraps of leather and wool, wearing carved wooden mummers' masks depicting the [personae](../../mixed/magic/lore/Dramaturgy.md#the-personae) as wild beasts, fight with the Marchers. They rarely speak during battle, inspiring by their presence and their wild abandon. If they are disappointed by the [careful strategy](../../out-of-character/rules/Army_orders.md#steady-conquest) employed by the Marchers they do not show it. Armed with jagged polearms fashioned in echo of farming tools, they look like scarecrows but fight with the passionate fire only the servants of the *Ranting Tumult* can deploy.

Between the fighting they share the campfires, moving through the army so that no one of them spends two nights with the same group. They sing, they dance, they tell tall tales, they always have strange liquor and rarer substances. And they urge the warriors of the Marches to embrace their passions, to follow their hearts, to give voice to their dissatisfaction.

Perhaps the presence of Janon's chosen in the Tusks is not an unqualified boon. There are particular grumbles about the fact that the [Quartermaster General](../../mixed/titles/Quartermaster_General_of_the_Imperial_Armies.md) chose not to allocate shares of the [Guerdon](../../out-of-character/rules/Military_unit.md#guerdon-shares) to the war to reclaim their homes. This speaks well of those [captains](../../out-of-character/rules/Military_unit.md) who fight with the Tusks regardless, speaks well of their Pride and Loyalty. What would [Tom Drake](../nations/varushka/Life_of_Tom_Drake.md) say about a [Military Council](../../mixed/government/military/Military_Council.md) that holds such a tight grasp on their purse-strings when there is fighting to be done? As they say, you don't own it unless you can defend it. Should the Empire's soldiers not receive just payment for their labour? Is their pay a gift, to be given and withheld at the whim of far-away generals from other nations? It's not a new complaint, but with the Fire-Crow's encouragement it is voiced out loud by more soldiers than one might expect.

Just as well, perhaps, that the heralds of Janon are too busy fighting and stirring the passions of the Tusks to talk to the farmers or the miners of the Mournwold as well, perhaps.

Either way, there are no grand battles, just a hard job that needs doing and is done professionally, without fuss nor muss. More orcs fall than Marchers, but blood is spilled on both sides. War is a thrice-ploughed field, after all. The general has left strict orders not to engage the Jotun at Farstrider's Watch, and as the Summer Solstice approaches the Chalkdowns is Imperial once more. The Jotun are penned in at Farstrider's Watch, but show no sign of abandoning the castle or the [Greensward](../nations/the-marches/Mournwold.md#greensward), or the [Singing Caves](../../mixed/titles/Singing_Caves.md). The work is not done, not yet.

## The Rumbling Fens

When the Spring Equinox dawned, the Jotun held almost the entirety of Bregasland. Their [sudden, overwhelming attack](If_one_be_gone_we_carry_on.md) had expanded their dominion from [Graven Rock](../nations/the-marches/Bregasland.md#graven-rock) across the marshes and fens in a single fell swoop. They had reckoned without the [Strong Reeds](../../mixed/government/military/Strong_Reeds.md), however, and the stubborn resistance of the Bregas.

Through the [Sentinel Gate](../world/Sentinel_Gate.md), the heroes of the Empire interceded against the forces [marching north](If_one_be_gone_we_carry_on.md#glory-at-the-end-battle-opportunity) from [Sallow](../nations/the-marches/Bregasland.md#the-rushes) along toward [Ottery](../nations/the-marches/Bregasland.md#ottermire). Under the [guidance](../../mixed/government/military/Field_Marshal.md) of the ‘Reeds general herself, that intervention was a victory for the Empire. The Jotun advance was [blocked](If_one_be_gone_we_carry_on.md#objective-block-the-sallow-way); the pennants of the thieving Corazón were [seized](If_one_be_gone_we_carry_on.md#battlefield-opportunity-capture-the-corazons-pennants); the bounds were [beaten](If_one_be_gone_we_carry_on.md#objective-rouse-the-strong-reeds) and the Strong Reeds inspired, fired up to take the battle to the invaders.

The reward for a good job is more work, of course, and in the wake of that victory, the Strong Reeds begin making preparations, rallying those who oppose the Jotun, laying in supplies, readying to strike back with overwhelming force when the order is finally given. Bregasland will not find them slow to respond when the call goes out.

> *In-character text:*
>
> > *In-character text:*
> >
> > "The Reward for hard work is more work". We lay low again for one more season. But do you hear amongst the Rushes? All around the Empire, the home of the Strong Reeds is toasted, and on the battlefield our beaters sing your virtue, your pride and loyalty. We who are never bent or broken, we who defeated "Queen" Fisher, we who stood against Jotun Steel. Your Loyalty to our land is met with loyalty from the citizens of the Empire. So cleave to your Loyalty, the loyalty of Tom Drake and Farstrider. Cleave to your pride of Guildenstern and Hooke. So I ask you for one more season, one more grind, then we will stand again stronger than ever.
> >
> > Amberlain P. Black, General of the Strong Reeds

For their part the Jotun rally, recover from their losses, adjust their plans and sweep into Ottermire without pause. The town falls in less than an hour. The Jotun offer the Choice, and the whole of Bregasland is theirs. Or, at least, the land is theirs. The people are another matter, however. The Corazón have been bested, and the Empire has shown a willingness to fight those who dare to oppress their people. It sends a powerful message to those across the territory; that the Bregas and their Imperial neighbours are tied together by bonds of [Loyalty](../religion/Loyalty.md) that, while frayed in places, still hold strong. The sullen mood that has simmered in Bregasland since the magistrates "failed" to [execute the Droverbills](../winds-of-fortune/Despise_treachery.md#the-droverbill-clan-of-grey-fens) has subsided. If someone tries to claim the Empire doesn't care about the Marches, they'll be given short shrift.

That's not to say that everything is green and fragrant among the Bregas. Copies of the [Life of Tom Drake](../nations/varushka/Life_of_Tom_Drake.md) begin to circulate in the conquered land. Even though the Jotun rarely care what their thralls read, hand-written copies are made by rushlight, passed quietly from hand to hand in the back rooms of pubs and stuffed into sacks of animal feed sold at the markets. The part that people are most interested in is that Good Old Tom - who was alright for a ‘Wolder, the one good one - was Loyal to the Marches first and the Empire second. There was no guerdon in Tom's day, but doubtless he'd have had a few choice words for the anyone who said that soldiers fighting for the Marches would get nowt for all their hard work, and they'd not be "polite" words.

Yet there are more pressing concerns than Tom Drake, beloved as he is. The Jotun leave no room for doubt that the Dour Fens are theirs, now. They do not tolerate rebellion or resistance, and those who want to fight are forced to sneak away and join the Strong Reeds among the rushes. Hated traitor Stephen of Sarcombe is sent to [Eel's Landing](../nations/the-marches/Bregasland.md#eels-landing) to explain to the Greywaters that they had best mind their manners or they will pay a hard price. Steward Margery, sadly, is nowhere to be found and neither are most of the house. They have disappeared into the marshes with the same practiced ease that made them so useful to the Strong Reeds when the Fishers came to tell the Bregas they were part of her new kingdom. Stephen is apparently furious, but then when is he not? Given his head, he would probably have burned Eel's Landing to the ground - but Queen Yrsa Jansdóttir, Jarl of Jarls, general of the Lion of the North, keeps him on a short leash and will not let him exact vengeance against the people of Bregasland unless they take up arms against their new overlords.

For the most part, at least at the moment, the Jotun seem content to rule. Many of their warriors have marched south, but a strong core of half a dozen armies and several champion warbands remain in the marshes. Most of those champions support the Lion of the North, and that represents a problem. While the other armies complete the conquest of Bregasland, Yrsa sets her warriors and champions to [scour the marshes](../../mixed/government/military/Army_qualities.md#scouting) looking for any sign of organised resistance, for any sign of the Strong Reeds. A sizable bounty has been placed on the head of the general - but there is an even richer reward for anyone who can capture the leader of the ‘Reeds and deliver her alive into the hands of the Queen of Kalsea.

The Strong Reeds prove harder to root out than a [spy network](../../out-of-character/rules/Spy_network.md) or a ring of Grendel agents, but between the Spring Equinox and the Summer Solstice the Reeds must remain on their toes. Several times a warband or a cell of sympathisers need to be relocated with hours notice to avoid the lions of Queen Yrsa. There are victories as well - vicious fights between Jotun who manage to find some of the Marcher soldiers which leave both sides bloodied. It is a high-stakes game of cat and mouse, but for now the Reeds have the upper hand. As the Summer Solstice nears, the Jotun are in no doubt that the Strong Reeds are present, and that they are organising resistance against the orcs' rule. How much *else* they know is not clear, but it does not seem they know exactly where the Strong Reeds are - if they did they would have ended them already.

It's clear, though, that the Jotun seem to be planning to stay. [Igya Olgafsdottir](In_each_line_lies_another_line.md#objective-kill-igya-olgafsdottir), Jarl of [Tromsa](../barbarians/Tromsa.md), has apparently been spotted by Strong Reeds scouts visiting the Ice Fishers of Ldansk at the "[Burhfæst](Only_forward.md#object-halt-the-burhfæst)", the magical fortification at Graven Rock. Imperial prognosticators predict that the magical citadel is due to return to the realm of the [King-under-the-mountain](../eternals/Adamant.md) by the end of the Summer Solstice at the latest, but the presence of the master engineer and castle-builder in Bregasland may be a cause for alarm.

For now, though, Bregasland is as quiet as a conquered Marcher territory ever is. Yet it is clear to everyone, Marcher and Jotun alike, that this situation will not, cannot, last. The pebbles are already bouncing, the avalanche is inevitable.

## Participation

- **Characters whose military unit supported the Tusks or the Strong Reeds may partake of a form of spiritual inspiration**
- **Accessing this inspiration requires an anointing of either Loyalty or Pride**
- **The inspiration is stronger for Marcher characters but people from other nations still receive a benefit**

Military units who supported the Strong Reeds or the Tusks may receive a special benefit
The Empire has taken steps on several battles to bolster the [Pride](../religion/Pride.md) and [Loyalty](../religion/Loyalty.md) of the Marcher armies, and the people of Mournwold and Bregasland. The generals of the Strong Reeds and Tusks have sworn powerful oaths to protect the people of "their" territory. The inspiration these acts provide should not be underestimated.

Any Marcher character whose [military unit](../../out-of-character/rules/Military_unit.md) supported either the Tusks or the Strong Reeds this season may experience that inspiration if it suits their characterisation. As long as they have an [anointing](../../out-of-character/rules/Anointing.md) of [Loyalty](../../mixed/magic/lore/Auras_of_Loyalty.md#anointing) or [Pride](../../mixed/magic/lore/Auras_of_Pride.md#anointing), they receive a bonus [hero point](../../out-of-character/rules/Heroic_skills.md#hero) during the Summer Solstice. This bonus increases their maximum number of hero points for the duration of the summit.

Any character who is not a Marcher may also receive a benefit but it is less intense. Instead of an increase to their maximum hero points for the summit, they receive a single temporary hero point which is lost once it is used - provided they have an appropriate anointing.

Note that the character does not have to start the event with the anointing of Pride or Loyalty. They can claim the benefit if they receive that aura in play *provided* it is a [Marcher](../nations/the-marches/The_Marches.md) priest who leads the ceremony. In the event that they lose or change their anointing of Pride or Loyalty, the bonus hero point is lost and can't be regained during the summit.

### Customs (Mournwold)

- **Small but significant numbers of Marcher orcs have been taking up arms in Marcher armies and to defend the Mournwold**
- **Two groups of Marcher orcs are being hunted by Ulvenwar forces in Greensward**

For the past [few months](If_one_be_gone_we_carry_on.md), Marcher army recruiters have been dealing with unprecedented times. Faced with the invasion of their homeland, dozens of Marcher orc citizens have been abandoning their pacifist ways and taking up arms in the defence of their nation and the Empire. They have proven brave and capable soldiers, if inexperienced, and exclaim a fervent willingness to fight and die for the land as befits any right-thinking Marcher. They are still a minority compared to those who continue to till the soil and beat the bounds without picking up weapons, but their ranks are growing among the troops of the four armies.

The rest of the Marches appear to have welcomed this development with open arms. But, as is often the case, thorny religious questions arise. The Marcher orcs who take up arms and seek to fight and die in battle claim that by doing so, they are giving up their chance at reincarnation granted to their pacifist fellows. Clearly, this resembles the Jotun religious beliefs; understandable, since almost all of the Marcher orcs grew up as Jotun subjects. But those orcs of the Mourn who felt the call of battle went west years ago. These orcs taking up the billhook don't talk of seeking a glorious death in battle. They talk of making a sacrifice *in defence of the land*. They make the choice to fight, and they will be judged by the red-clad orcs of the west who came before them to see if they are worthy of crossing the abyss. Or, so they say.

Whatever anyone thinks of the matter, Marcher orcs are fighting and dying for the cause, and believe they are risking their souls in the process. In the Mournwold, in Greensward, Mourn orcs are caught up in the fighting; some fighters, but some not.

## Breaking Burhfæst (Battle Opportunity)

Even though the Strong Reeds continue to hide across the wide fens, the barbarians are able to act here without contention, moving where they wish, and consolidating their positions. The farms, the scattered gardens, and the cottage workshops are now all under Jotun rule. Stores are taken to support the warriors, blacksmiths turned to support the repair of armour and sharpening of weapons. Barns turned into lodgings. Bregasland is now fully controlled by the Jotun.

One year ago Adamant sent a might Mountain Bulwark to join *Faraldi Blutesson* and the Ice Fishers at Graven Rock. There, the elderly ghodi invoked a boon from the *King Under the Mountain* and sent the guardian through the *Graven Door*, returning the *Burhfæst* from the summer realm. During the last twelve months the mighty fortress has stood sentinel in eastern Bregasland, offering the Jotun a fine vantage point looking across Mitwold and the Mourn. The warband from Ldansk have remained inside the *Burhfæst*, and no effort has been taken by the Empire to assail the fortification. Adamant's boon was only a gift for a year and a day, and when the sun sets on the solstice the white granite walls will once again depart to the summer realm.

Yet, watchful eyes in Bregasland have informed the war scouts of a Jotun force heading to the fort, escorting another vast guardian of King Adamant's realm towards Graven Rock. The behemoth has made a path eastwards, smashing through any obstacle in the way. With it come a great number of Jotun warriors in high spirits and filled with purpose, these are some of the orcs that swiftly brought an end to Imperial control of Ottery and the Bregas end of the Otterway. Prognosticators have determined that the guardian can be intercepted on the Saturday of the solstice as it marches along the *Cinder Drove*. This offers an opportunity to defeat the guardian before it leaves The Rushes and arrives at the foot of Graven Rock.

### Objective: Ensure the *Burhfæst's* Dismissal

- **Stop the procession of the Mountain Bulwark towards Graven Rock**
- **Incapacitate the guardian and then destroy it with a powerful ritual**
- **If not stopped then the *Burhfæst's* enchantment will endure for another year upon Graven Rock**

The Mountain Bulwark, one of Adamant's colossal summer guardians, are massive walking mountains of enduring stone. They have fists of hardened granite that can smash foes to the ground, splinter weapons or shields, and cast soldiers through the air with ease. Hewn from the deepest stones in Adamant's domain, these creatures are nigh on impossible to stop and pose a serious risk to Imperial lives should it break the line of battle. Last time one of these creatures headed to Graven Rock it was accompanied by a band of summerborn champions who drew inspiration and fighting prowess from its presence.

To defeat the monster it must first be incapacitated by the blows of the Empire's warriors. Only then will it be possible to destroy it - by casting one of the destructive rituals of spring or winter - [Rising Roots that Rend Stone](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Rising_Roots_that_Rend_Stone.md) or [Inevitable Collapse into Ruin](../../mixed/magic/rituals/Inevitable_Collapse_into_Ruin.md). These rituals won't harm it while it is still active. Given the importance of the summer guardian to the Jotun, it is certain that they would not simply abandon it into the Empire's hands and will make every attempt to restore its vitality and continue the march to Graven Rock.

### Objective: Save the Clakes

- **Rescue the Clake family from Jarl Aertha to lift the resentful mood in The Rushes**

When Mathilda Fisher sought to become Steward of Bregasland, the Clakes were quick to throw in their lot and join her council. Tattling on their neighbours who had long disdained them, the family were well rewarded for their help. Following the downfall of the Fishers, the Clakes paid a heavy price as stolen possessions were recovered and grudges settled. Thirty-six were sent to the gallows once the magistrates were done. Yet Sammy Clakes and the core of his inner circle and immediate family escaped the noose. They went to ground out in the sodden expanses of The Rushes. Mistrust and a grim-faced cynicism holds fast in The Rushes.

Last season there was word that the Clakes had welcomed the return of the Jotun to Bregasland and that Sammy Clake had met with the traitor Stephen of Sarcombe to join his cause. If so, it appears someone has recently had a change of heart. Details are scant, but the rumour is that the orcs asked something of the Clakes they were not prepared to give. Truth is, nobody knows why, but Jarl Aertha has spent the last month searching for them on command of the Jarl-of-Jarls. Now she has caught them, she has bound the Clakes in rope and is leading them to the *Burhfæst*. The orcs give short shrift to criminals and turncoats and it is highly likely they will be executed by the Jotun.

If this group of Clakes are executed, then the current resentful mood in the Rushes will rumble on unchanged. The ill feelings will endure with the family's cousins and their cousins' cousins. No-one nurses a grudge like a Marcher. But if the Empire rescued the Clakes, it would be bound to put a few noses out of joint (they're not popular after all), but it would put a stop to the current bitterness, permanently ending the resentful mood in the region.

### Battlefield Opportunity: Bring in Sammy Clakes

- **Freeing the Clakes will see Sammy turn himself in**
- **The ringleader can be returned to the magistrates in Anvil to face the law**
- **If Sammy Clake is brought to justice it will draw a line under the whole sorry affair with the Clakes**

Sammy Clake is not among Aertha's captives. Surprisingly, he has sent a missive to the Empire desperately seeking help to rescue his household. He is offering his own head if the Empire will save his family members. The head of the Clakes is ultimately responsible for the actions the family took during the Steward's Invasion. His request is simple, free the family and see them set free, in return he has promised to turn himself in on the battlefield.

If Sammy Clake can be brought in, he will face trial for the Clake's support for Mathilda Fisher and the misdemeanours of the family. He will face whatever punishment may be laid down, even though that is most certainly a quick execution. This will draw a line under the whole sorry matter and ensure everyone can move on. The local populace will come to remember a family saved by the heroes of the Marches, an enemy denied a poignant and symbolic victory, and the head of a household who saw saving his kin as being more important than keeping his own head. It's far from perfect, but as Clake himself writes, "There's two ways to bury a hatchet".
