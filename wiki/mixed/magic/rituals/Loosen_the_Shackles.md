# Loosen the Shackles

*[Image: This ritual is part of Urizen lore rather than Imperial lore .](https://www.profounddecisions.co.uk/empire-wiki/File:NBUrizen.jpg)*

> **Interdiction**  
> While [Agramant](../../../in-character/eternals/Agramant.md) is subject to the [enmity](../../../in-character/eternals/Alignment.md#enmity) of the [Conclave](../../government/conclave/Conclave.md), it is illegal to perform this ritual.

## Rules

### Winter Magnitude 120

### Urizen Lore

This ritual is part of [Urizen lore](../../../in-character/nations/urizen/Urizen_magical_traditions.md#urizen-lore) rather than Imperial lore. Any Urizen character with the appropriate lore can master or [perform](../../../out-of-character/rules/Rituals.md#performing-a-ritual) this ritual. A character from another nation who mastered the ritual before it became part of Urizen lore may still perform it, but does so under the [usual rules](../../../out-of-character/rules/Formulaic_ritual.md#ritual-texts) for performing a ritual learned from a ritual text.

### Performing the Ritual

Performing this ritual takes at least 10 minutes of roleplaying. If the ritual is cast using the [Imperial Regio](Regio.md#the-imperial-regio) it requires at least 5 minutes of roleplaying instead. This ritual targets an [Imperial army](../../../out-of-character/rules/Army.md). The [general](../../government/military/Military_Council.md) responsible for the army (or the [egregore](../../../out-of-character/crew/Egregores.md) if the general is not at Anvil) must be present throughout. The target of the ritual must be cut with an edged weapon until they bleed, and the performance must involve the evocation of Agramant by at least three of his commonly-used sobriquets.

During the ritual the casters must be in a strong Winter [regio](Regio.md).

This ritual is an [enchantment](../../../out-of-character/rules/Enchantment.md). A target may only be under one enchantment effect at a time.

### Effects

The ritual draws on the power of [Agramant](../../../in-character/eternals/Agramant.md), and fills an army with unquenchable hunger to kill as many of their foes as possible. This raises the strength of the army by 4,000 additional soldiers, but only for purposes of inflicting casualties. As with any enchantment on an army or navy, the additional army strength granted by this enchantment may be reduced by the [orders](../../../out-of-character/rules/War.md#orders) issued by the [general](../../titles/General.md). While the effect is expressed as being equivalent to additional soldiers, this enchantment never reduces [casualties](../../../out-of-character/rules/Casualties.md#calculating-casualties) suffered by the army.

While the enchantment persists, the general responsible for the army experiences a [roleplaying effect](../../../out-of-character/rules/Roleplaying_effects.md): *It feels easy, and natural, to kill or maim people you don't particularly like. It's easy to consider the lives of people you feel no affection for as entirely disposable. When in the presence of someone you actively dislike, you are assailed by visions of their messy death at your hand; dealing with such people becomes actively difficult. The more you dislike someone, the easier it is to imagine killing them in cold blood.*

The powerful urge the enchantment creates can cause unpredictable additional effects on those fighting alongside the army, especially those with the [draughir](../lore/Draughir.md) [lineage](../lore/Lineage.md). Such effects are unpredictable; when they occur they are described in the appropriate Winds of War.

The effect lasts until the start of the next Profound Decisions Empire event.

### Assurance

This ritual permanently loses all power if all the Empire's armies go for a year and a day without shedding the blood of an enemy.

## Description

This ritual spreads quickly through the soldiers of an army, reducing their capacity to empathise with their enemies. It is subtle enough that the army does not immediately turn on itself, but the captains of any soldiers under this enchantment would do well to separate any of its members who actively dislike each other. While warriors are no strangers to killing their enemies, an army enchanted with *Loosen the Shackles* is dangerous because all of its members are possessed of an unquenchable thirst for the blood of their foes - metaphorically and in some cases literally.

The enchantment draws on resonances of [cruelty](../../../out-of-character/rules/Winter_magic.md#cruelty), but also the [hunger](../../../out-of-character/rules/Winter_magic.md#hunger) for victory. There are notes in the original text that, in times and places when [Agramant](../../../in-character/eternals/Agramant.md) is not under [enmity](../../../in-character/eternals/Alignment.md#enmity), he sometimes sends heralds or Winter beasts to fight alongside an army enchanted with this ritual. In theory, Agramant could send some [drudges](../../../in-character/eternals/Inhabitants_of_the_realms.md#drudges) or [champions](../../../in-character/eternals/Inhabitants_of_the_realms.md#champions) to accompany an army in spite of the enmity of the Conclave - this is a ritual summoning after all - but in practice it is hard to predict exactly how *Blood-on-the-Snow* might choose to react to the invocation of this potent enchantment. On several occasions, there is talk of Agramant "rewarding" a general who makes particularly good use of the bloodthirsty power the ritual provides with a weapon that further exacerbates their thirst for victory and death.

*Loosen the Shackles* [entered Urizen lore](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/Shake_it_off.md#bad-lore-and-black-drops) shortly before the Winter Solstice 384YE, as part of a book entitled *[Ceremonies of the Black Drop Society](../../../in-character/eternals/Agramant.md#black-drop-society)*. The attendants of the [Great Library](../../../in-character/eternals/Phaleron.md) were deeply uncomfortable with the rituals in that book and their presence among Urizen lore, more due to their illegal nature than any sentimental concerns about their origin. They were apparently all submitted at the same time as [Treacher's Quill](Treacher-s_Quill.md), but Phaleron itself intervened to delay their appearance in lore while it pondered how best to respond. In the end it decided to do the only reasonable thing; present the rituals to the Urizen as part of their lore as agreed when the lore was established. The Celestial Library does not enjoy causing trouble for its Urizeni allies, but the [Gift of Knowledge](Gift_of_Knowledge.md) is available to every Imperial citizen; it cannot pick and choose who can contribute rituals via the ritual.

## Common Elements

The text mentions that this ritual has obvious resonance with the [astronomantic](../lore/Astronomancy.md) constellation of [the Claw](../lore/The_Claw.md). In [dramaturgy](../lore/Dramaturgy.md), a particularly vicious or brutal version of [the Captain](../lore/The_Captain.md) or [the Doctor](../lore/The_Doctor.md) might be employed, along with a story of murder or mayhem making liberal use of [the Blade](../lore/The_Blade.md) and [the Battlefield](../lore/The_Battlefield.md). A dramaturgical ritual might also invoke the rarely-employed figure of [the Beast](../lore/Dramaturgy.md#the-beast), symbolising the way this ritual draws power directly from Agramant.

Some [blood magicians](../lore/Blood_magic.md) are made especially uncomfortable by the way this ritual requires a version of blood magic. The general may be willing, thus avoiding the breaking of the tradition taboo, but there is a sense that the practice is intended to somehow taint or subvert the "pure" practice of their art.
