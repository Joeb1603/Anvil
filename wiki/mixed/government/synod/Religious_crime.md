# Religious crime

### Abuse of Powers

The crime of abuse of powers is defined as: *The misuse, or abuse, of the powers of a priest. This includes the powers of the Synod, as well as liao ceremonies.*

The Synod has proscribed the following as abuse of powers:

| Subject | Proscription raised by | Date | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Performing the ceremony of [excommunication](../../../out-of-character/rules/Excommunication.md) without a [writ](Writ_of_Excommunication.md), where the subject does not consent[(1)](#1) | [Tribune of the Synod](../../titles/Tribune_of_the_Synod.md) | [387YE Winter](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/387YE_Winter_Solstice_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-4), amended [388YE Spring](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/388YE_Spring_Equinox_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-80) | [link](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/Stones_in_the_wellspring.md#abuse-of-powers) |

1. This does not include using the ceremony to *remove* an existing excommunication.

### Blasphemy

The crime of blasphemy is defined as: *The denigration of the paragons and the paths of virtue. This includes promoting false virtues and the teachings of false exemplars or false paragons.*

The false virtues are understood to be Anarchy (also known as Freedom), Peace (also known as Understanding), Hatred (also known as Purity) and Vengeance (also known as Justice).

The Synod has proscribed the following as acts of blasphemy:

### Desecration

The crime of desecration is defined as: *The removal of spontaneously created virtuous auras such as legacies of ascendance to paragonhood. This includes such auras arising on areas and objects, and those people who do not wished them removed.*

The Synod has proscribed the following as acts of desecration:

| Subject | Proscription raised by | Date | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Desecration or destruction of any relics or memorials associated with a paragon or exemplar[(1)](#1) | [Tribune of the Synod](../../titles/Tribune_of_the_Synod.md) | [387YE Winter](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/387YE_Winter_Solstice_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-6) | [link](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/Stones_in_the_wellspring.md#desecration) |

1. This only applies where the relic or memorial bears a spontaneous aura.

### Heresy

The crime of heresy is defined as: *The wilful rejection, or perversion of, the orthodox [Doctrines of the Faith](../../../in-character/religion/The_Doctrines_of_the_Faith.md) as laid down by the Imperial Synod, or actively teaching and promoting false doctrines.*

The Synod does permit the discussion, and debate, of the doctrines, and potential variations thereof, as part of theological investigation and learning. The criminal act is the teaching of “truth” that has not been recognized as such by the Synod. Virtuous priests are admonished to seek to change doctrine in the Synod, rather than undermine it.

The Synod has proscribed the following as heresy:

| Subject | Proscription raised by | Date | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Excessionism](../../../in-character/religion/Schisms_and_Heresies.md#the-excessionist-heresy), the belief that too much of a virtue can be as bad as too little | [Tribune of the Synod](../../titles/Tribune_of_the_Synod.md) | [387YE Winter](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/387YE_Winter_Solstice_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-7) | [link](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/Stones_in_the_wellspring.md#heresy) |

### Idolatry

The crime of idolatry is defined as: *Submitting mortal will to any force or sapient being not otherwise defined as a person in law. This includes worship, exaltation, or devotion towards any such being or force.*

The definition of idolatry was [changed](../../../in-character/senate-sessions/388YE_Summer_Solstice_Senate_sessions.md#redefine-crime-of-idolatry) at the Summer Solstice 388YE, following [calls](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/Stones_in_the_wellspring.md#a-change-in-perspective) by the Prosperity Assembly to amend it to include orcs.

The Synod has proscribed the following as acts of idolatry:

| Subject | Proscription raised by | Date | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entering into the service of an eternal or tulpa, where the service subsumes the individual's free will[(2)](#2) | Eevi of the Gadrai | [387YE Winter](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/387YE_Winter_Solstice_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-145), amended [388YE Summer](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/388YE_Summer_Solstice_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-160) | [Initial proscription](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/Stones_in_the_wellspring.md#idolatry) <br> [Subsequent amendment](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/A_little_at_a_time.md#idolatry) |
| Venerating an icon of an eternal | Felix Saltspire | [387YE Winter](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/387YE_Winter_Solstice_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-128) | [link](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/Stones_in_the_wellspring.md#idolatry) |
| Ending a person's life, or allowing a person's life to end, as an offering to an inhuman power | Sofonisba Amilcara of the City Below | [388YE Spring](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/388YE_Spring_Equinox_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-66) |  |
| Venerating or entering the service of an inhuman entity of the labyrinth | Lepidina | [388YE Spring](../../../in-character/synod-judgements/388YE_Spring_Equinox_Synod_judgements.md#judgement-149) |  |

1. This does not include serving an eternal as their representative to the Empire, so long as this is public knowledge.

### Moral injury

The crime of moral injury is defined as: *An act via physical, spiritual, or magical that directly harms, corrupts or defiles the soul of an Imperial citizen.*

Moral injury was [created](../../../in-character/senate-sessions/388YE_Summer_Solstice_Senate_sessions.md#create-crime-of-moral-injury) as a religious crime by the Senate at the Summer Solstice 388YE. The Constitutional Court has [published guidance](../../../in-character/winds-of-fortune/A_little_at_a_time.md#moral-injury) as to what could be validly proscribed under this law.

The Synod has not yet proscribed any acts as moral injury.
